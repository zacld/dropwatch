Skiddle buying architecture

How a Skiddle purchase works from release to confirmation, read from Skiddle's public pages, help centre and one live event page (no purchases made). Everything up to payment can be automated reliably. Payment is the bottleneck: Skiddle's website does not keep your card, so a fully unattended purchase needs Skiddle credit or a remembered PayPal login.

Easy: reading tiers and prices

Every ticket type is published in the page's structured data with exact name, price, and in-stock or sold-out status. No scraping of the visual layout needed, and Skiddle's API is not needed either.

Reliable: queue and selection

A fair queue with random places, the same device keeps its place, and a simple ticket form with stable IDs and quantity dropdowns, posted once.

Hard: payment, 8-minute window

Website card details are deleted after each purchase, so a card must be typed in every time. Saved cards exist only in Skiddle's app, and still need the security code.

How the ticket form works

  • One form that posts to /whatson/tickets_check.php.
  • Each ticket type is a row with a stable ID (e.g. ticket-2451188) and a quantity dropdown, here up to 8. Sold-out rows have no dropdown.
  • Each row shows face value ("£51.95 +bf") and the all-in price (£59.22).
  • The button reads "£0.00 - Pay" until tickets are chosen, then leads to checkout.
  • The visual styling uses generated class names that change, so Dropwatch should match rows by ticket ID and name, never by styling.

A design fix this revealed

One list mixes four kinds of ticket:

  • Release tiers: First, Second, Third, Fourth Release.
  • Entry-time tickets: "Pre-1pm entry", cheapest, but you must arrive before 1pm.
  • Bundles: "4 for 3", quantity 4 only.
  • VIP.

"Allow any eligible tier" would wrongly pick "Pre-1pm entry" as the cheapest. The tier instruction must stay within one ticket family chosen by the customer (e.g. General Admission releases).

The best handoff: prepare the basket, pay on the phone

Dropwatch does everything except the payment itself, then hands the ready basket to the customer's phone:

  1. The cloud buyer waits in the queue, selects the authorised tickets, fills contact details, answers refund protection and ticks the terms.
  2. Push alert: "Tickets held: 2 × First Release, £84.24. Tap to pay. 7:30 left."
  3. The Dropwatch app opens that same checkout on the phone, and the customer pays with Apple Pay, Google Pay or their card. With Apple Pay that's one Face ID tap.
  4. Dropwatch confirms the order from the result page and order history.

Technically the basket belongs to the browser session that created it, so the app must carry that session over to the phone. That transfer is the main thing to build and test. Fully unattended remains possible for customers who pay with Skiddle credit or, possibly, PayPal.

First live run: blocked at sign-in

On 5 Oct 2026 the Dropwatch buyer (Chrome controlled by Playwright, started by the account holder on their own computer) opened a real Skiddle event and was refused at sign-in with a 403 from Skiddle's CloudFront firewall. Skiddle detects automated browsers. Dropwatch does not disguise its buyer or copy a signed-in session into it to get around this, so automated checkout on Skiddle needs Skiddle's permission, for example recognising Dropwatch as an approved partner. Nothing was booked.

How to Dropwatch Skiddle reliably

  1. Watch the page, not the layout. Read tiers, prices and status from the structured data. That gives verified savings in Discovery too.
  2. Join the waiting room as soon as it opens, in the customer's single session, and stay in it. Never refresh repeatedly or open extra tabs.
  3. At the front, act in seconds. Match the authorised ticket by ID within its family, check the all-in total against the limits, choose quantity, press Pay once.
  4. Pay by the customer's chosen route: credit or PayPal unattended where possible; otherwise an instant "Needs You" alarm to type the card within the 8 minutes.
  5. Confirm from Order history. If the result is unclear, check orders and never submit again.
  6. If sold out: optionally join Skiddle's official Waiting List (up to 2 tickets). Released tickets are held for 20 minutes, or until 8:30am overnight, and Dropwatch can complete the claim.

Still unknown: next tests

  • Basket hand-over: whether a basket created by the cloud buyer opens on the customer's phone with time left, and whether Apple Pay shows there.
  • Skiddle credit when signed in: where the credit tick box appears in account checkout.
  • Credit: buy a small gift card, redeem it, and check whether it covers a whole order including fees.
  • PayPal: whether a signed-in PayPal session approves without interaction.
  • Bank approval (3-D Secure): how often it appears for card payments.
  • The queue page: what it looks like live, and whether it checks for automation.
  • API: Skiddle's API is read-only event search (with a "tickets available" filter); no buying. Useful for discovery only, and it needs written approval for commercial use.
