What it means

  • Ticketmaster is the widest single source (7 of 12), but it's also the hardest to get permission from.
  • Most UK series sell through several outlets at once. Parklife names 7 official outlets and Worried About Henry offers 4. The same tickets can often be bought somewhere more open than Ticketmaster.
  • Skiddle, Kaboodle and Resident Advisor each cover 2–4 venues or series. Skiddle's published terms don't mention automated buying, and it runs an API, an affiliate scheme and promoter tools.
  • Ibiza is fragmented: DC10 sells on DICE, Amnesia on CM.com, UNVRS on its own site.

Recommended order

  1. Skiddle first. It's the most open-looking: no automated-buying clause in its published terms, an API (commercial use by written approval from dev@skiddle.com), an affiliate scheme and promoter tools. It sells for Parklife and Worried About Henry, and reportedly for The Warehouse Project and Teletech. Ask for commercial API approval and whether customer-authorised checkout could be supported.
  2. The Warehouse Project family as a promoter partner. WHP runs or co-runs You&Me, Steel City Dance Discs, Teletech London and WAH x WHP. One promoter relationship covers several of your priority nights, whichever platform they sell on.
  3. Kaboodle for Drumsheds, Worried About Henry and Parklife. Its website terms have no automated-buying clause, but its full agent terms haven't been read yet.
  4. Resident Advisor for fabric. Its terms ban unauthorised automated access, so written authorisation is the only route.
  5. Ticketmaster stays the long-term target (7 of 12), pursued through its developer programme in parallel.
  6. Ibiza later. DICE, CM.com and UNVRS's own checkout are three separate integrations for one season.
